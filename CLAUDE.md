# frame-tv-art-sync

A Python CLI that curates and controls Art Mode on a Samsung Frame TV over the LAN. It mirrors a Google Photos album onto the TV, and drives art mode, brightness, slideshow, and mattes.

Target hardware is a `QN32LS03CB`, the 2023 LS03C generation at 32". Panel specs, Art Mode sensor behavior, pairing steps, power measurements, and physical build notes all live in `docs/initial research.md`. Read that before touching TV code; it is the accumulated research and it is not repeated here.

This is intended to be open sourced, so keep the TV host, album URL, and anything else account-specific out of the repo.


## Scope

### MVP

1. `frame pair` -- first-run token handshake, run interactively once.
1. `frame sync` -- mirror a link-shared Google Photos album onto the TV. `--dry-run` prints the plan without touching anything, and it's what you want before the first real run, since this and `frame bakeoff` are the two commands that delete. `--label` draws the crop rule that fired onto each photo, which is how crop rules get judged off the panel.
1. `frame art-mode on|off`
1. `frame brightness N`
1. `frame slideshow 0` -- turning a slideshow off is the only part of this the firmware accepts. Starting one is refused, so the `N`, `--ordered` and `--category` half of the original scope isn't buildable; `docs/spikes.md` T5 has what was tried.
1. `frame mattes` to list the types the TV draws around each image shape, and the colors. There is no command that applies one: a matte can only be set at upload time, because `change_matte()` does nothing on this firmware, so changing one is editing `config.toml` and running `frame sync`, which replaces every photo whose rendering no longer matches. `docs/spikes.md` T14 has why the cheap version isn't available.
1. `frame status` -- current artwork, art mode state, and inventory summary.
1. `frame bakeoff --compare=colors|types [--orientation=landscape|portrait]` -- one photo per shape in the album, uploaded once per matte, so a mat can be chosen by looking at the panel rather than at a name. `--orientation` narrows a round; without it a round covers every shape. `--clear` on its own empties the TV, `--dry-run` prints the plan, and `--yes` skips the question. See **The bakeoff** below.

### Fast follow

Neither of these is MVP, and both come out of the same hole: nothing this tool does ever changes what the panel shows, and a slideshow can be turned off and never on.

* **Rotation driven off-TV, through `select_image(content_id)` on a timer.** The TV's own menu bottoms out at three minutes, and the API can't beat it, because `set_slideshow_status` takes its duration in whole minutes and refuses every non-zero one anyway. So a 30 second interval has to come from something outside the TV picking the next image, and `select_image` is the only candidate. It has never been called here. In its favor, a select is a request on the art channel that's already open rather than a new TCP connection like an upload, so the T18 suspect doesn't apply; against it, nothing says how the Art app holds up under thousands of selects a day, which is what T20 is about. `docs/TODO.md` T21 comes first either way, since a slideshow set by hand may persist and cover the three-minute case for free.
* **Organization, which lives in the inventory rather than on the TV.** Nothing on the TV can be organized: uploads all land in `MY-C0002`, `samsungtvws` reads a `category_id` on each item and exposes nothing that creates one, and the favourites substitute is gone since `set_favourite` fails on this firmware. Whether the SmartThings app can create an album on this model is unverified, and it's a phone check the size of T8. None of that blocks anything, though, because once rotation is driven off-TV the subset being rotated over is chosen locally, and the inventory already records which source each `content_id` came from. The TV never needs to know.


### Future, in rough priority order

* **Nightly on/off schedule:** two `launchd` plists calling `frame art-mode`. The TV has no clock-based scheduling of its own, so this has to live off-TV. Night Mode is an ambient-light proxy rather than a clock, so the TV stays lit as long as a lamp is on.
* **Local folder source:** point at a directory and upload its contents.
* **Museum art source:** CC0 art from the Art Institute of Chicago, Rijksmuseum, and the Met over IIIF, which is what replaces the Art Store subscription.
* **Automated sync via a Cloudflare Worker.** See the constraint below, because the Worker can fetch and prep but can never push to the TV.


## Architecture

Three layers, so that adding a photo source never touches TV code.

1. **Sources** return a list of items, each with a stable source-side id and a way to fetch bytes. Google album now; folder and museum art later.
1. **Pipeline** turns bytes into a TV-ready JPEG: sRGB, quality 95, highlights pulled down about 10% because the matte film blooms bright whites, the crop `[[pipeline.crop]]` calls for, and the frame bounded to fit inside `1920x1080` without ever being upscaled. The 32" is FHD, not 4K, despite what some retail listings say. **With no crop rule matching, nothing is cropped, whatever the shape** -- a 4:3 goes up at 1440x1080 and a 3:4 at 810x1080, both keeping their own aspect ratio, and the TV frames each one whole inside its own mat. No mat is ever drawn into a JPEG here, because one burned in is permanent and can never be restyled. Framing is therefore split between two things that behave differently: `[art.matte_by_ratio]` can be changed by re-uploading, while a crop taken in the pipeline is permanent in the stored bytes. A type whose aperture is fixed doesn't crop to fit -- it refuses to draw and crashes Art Mode, which is why the matte key is the image's ratio and not its orientation; `flexible` and `shadowbox` are the types whose aperture takes the image's own shape. **Crop before bound, never after**, or a 4:3 arriving at 1920x1440 would be shrunk to 1440x1080 and then cropped to 1440x810, handing the panel an upscale of resolution it had already thrown away. **And choose the matte after the crop, never before**, because the crop is what decides the shape the TV is handed: `syncer.planned_shape()` is the one place that prediction is made, so the diff and the upload cannot arrive at different answers.
1. **TV wrapper** is a thin layer over `samsungtvws`, so every firmware quirk and WebSocket workaround sits in one file.

**Inventory** is a local JSON file mapping each TV `content_id` to the source that uploaded it, that source's item id, and the settings the copy was rendered with. The first two are what make mirroring possible at all: without them you cannot tell your own uploads apart from art added by hand, and three months later you will create duplicates. The third is what makes a photo's rendering changeable after the fact, since the TV can be asked what it holds but never how it was made. `render.py` owns that record.

`frame sync` is a three-way diff between the album, the inventory, and `art.available()`. Deletes are scoped to `content_id`s the inventory attributes to that source, so art added by hand or by another source is never touched unless `sync.delete_added_by_hand` says otherwise, and Samsung's own art is never touched at all. `sync.py` computes the plan and is pure; `syncer.py` carries it out, and it fetches and prepares every photo into a spool *before* opening the art channel, because the channel closes itself after about 25 seconds of silence and a live download between two uploads would eventually outlast that. `docs/inventory-and-sync.md` has the rest of the ordering and why each step is where it is.


## Design decisions

* **Python over Node:** `samsungtvws` is the reference implementation that the Node and Home Assistant projects all credit, and it has the most miles on it. The Art Mode channel is where the firmware pain lives, so it is worth being in the ecosystem with the accumulated fixes, even though the rest of the stack here is usually PHP and JS.
* **Shared album scraping over the Picker API:** Google removed the `photoslibrary.readonly`, `photoslibrary.sharing`, and `photoslibrary` scopes on 2025-03-31, and calls using them now return `403 PERMISSION_DENIED`. The [Picker API](https://developers.google.com/photos/picker/guides/get-started-picker) is the sanctioned replacement, but it is interactive by design: a user opens a Google-hosted picker and the app polls a session for the result. That can't be automated, so this project scrapes the JSON embedded in a link-shared album's public page instead. Nothing about that is official or documented, and Google can break it whenever they want, so treat a scraper failure as expected maintenance rather than a surprise. The upside is that there's no OAuth at all, and therefore no refresh token to expire.
* **Mirror over append-only:** removing a photo from the album removes it from the TV. Art Mode storage is only about 6 GB, and delete-as-you-go beats writing a cleanup script later.
* **A change in how a photo is rendered is detected; a change in the photo itself is not.** An inventory entry records the settings its copy was uploaded with -- the matte, the crop, the tone curve, the JPEG quality, and a pipeline version -- so editing `config.toml` and syncing replaces every photo the change touches. The crop is recorded by its effect rather than by the rule that produced it, so rewriting a `when` clause to catch the same photo the same way replaces nothing, and changing a `to` or an `anchor` replaces exactly the photos that rule reaches. It records nothing about the pixels that arrived from the source, so a photo edited in Google Photos after it was uploaded stays stale on the TV until it is removed from the album and added back. The asymmetry is deliberate: the settings are here to compare against, and the pixels would have to be fetched to compare at all. Editing in place is also rare next to adding and removing, and closing that half later is additive rather than a rewrite. `docs/inventory-and-sync.md` has the two candidate fingerprints and the probe that says whether the free one works.
* **Every recorded setting is compared, so any of them can cost a full re-upload.** Taking `jpeg_quality` from 95 to 90 replaces the whole album, the same as changing a matte would. The alternative -- comparing only what visibly reframes a photo -- would mean a setting that quietly doesn't apply until the next photo happens to be uploaded, which is the drift the record exists to remove. `frame sync --dry-run` prints the replace count and says which settings moved, and `sync.short_run` is how to try one on two photos rather than on all of them.
* **A replacement is not gated by the delete flags.** `sync.delete_removed_from_album` answers whether this tool may stop mirroring a photo, and a photo being re-rendered is still in the album, so its old copy comes down whatever the flags say. `sync.py`'s `SyncPlan` states the general rule: a list no flag names is acted on unconditionally.
* **Re-sharing the album is out of scope:** the inventory keys on Google's media id, and whether that id survives the album being unshared and re-shared under a new link is untested and staying that way, because testing it means breaking the current share link and re-pointing `config.toml`. If the id doesn't hold, the first run after a re-share sees every photo as new, so it re-uploads the album and deletes the previous copies, and every run after that is stable. That costs uploading time and no data, which is why it's cheaper to recognize than to prevent. The README's troubleshooting section describes the symptom.
* **Retrying is automatic, and it is scoped to the one failure a wait cures.** The TV needs about ten seconds to notice a client has gone, so two commands in a row fail the second one; `_connect` waits 25s and reconnects once for exactly that, and raises everything else straight away. `TvClientTooSoon` is what carries the distinction, because all four connect failures arrive from the library as one `ConnectionFailure` and `_unreachable()` already had to tell them apart to pick an error message. There is no flag. **Scoping it is what makes always-on free:** retrying costs nothing when the connection succeeds, since the wait sits in the `except` branch, and the cases it can't fix -- a TV that is off, a client on the wrong subnet, a prompt nobody accepted -- would only have been reported 25s later. An earlier design made this `--retry` and had a manual run fail fast, which was picking a side of a tradeoff rather than removing it; auto-detecting a tty was rejected then and is still the wrong shape, because it would change behavior the moment a manual run is piped somewhere.
* **A CLI over a daemon:** the TV keeps its own state after the script disconnects, including uploaded images, selection, per-image matte, slideshow interval, and brightness. So nothing has to stay running. Short-lived invocations also sidestep the art channel going zombie, which is the failure mode long-running processes hit.
* **`uv` over pip and venv**, with a `pyproject.toml`, so `uv tool install` is the install path for anyone else.
* **GPL-2.0-or-later.**


## Config

`config.toml` in the working directory, gitignored, holding the TV's host, the album URL, a matte per image orientation, and the crop rules. `frame --config <path>` points somewhere else, which is what a scheduled job needs. Paths inside it, the `samsungtvws` token file included, resolve against the config file's own directory rather than the working directory. `inventory.json` sits there too and is not a key, because there is no reason to name it and every reason for it to travel with whichever config a scheduled job was pointed at. A committed `config.example.toml` documents the shape.

**`[art.matte_by_ratio]` is keyed to the image's aspect ratio, and none of it is the API's `portrait_matte_id`.** That field is keyed to the *panel's* orientation and is inert here, so `mattes.INERT_PORTRAIT_MATTE_ID` is what gets sent for it and the image's own matte always goes through `matte_id`.

**The ratio is the key because that is what the TV keys on, and orientation was a proxy that held only by accident.** `modern`, `modernthin` and `modernwide` draw a mat whose aperture is a fixed 16:9, and the firmware refuses to draw one around any other shape -- a 4:3 landscape gets the same 40000 dialog a portrait does. Every fixed-aperture success on record turned out to be a 1920x1080 image, which is why "six types on a landscape" survived as long as it did. `mattes.FIXED_APERTURE_TYPES` may only go out at exactly `16:9` and `mattes.ACCEPTED_ON_ANY_SHAPE` covers the rest; `docs/spikes.md` T22 is the round that found it.

**A ratio is snapped through `Fraction.limit_denominator(16)` before anything looks it up**, so the album's `85/64`, `4/3` and `1599/1204` landscapes are all one `4:3` key rather than three names for a shape no eye can tell apart. That snapping is the only reason a ratio is writable in a config file. Keys are reduced too, so `16:10` and `8:5` are one key rather than two that shadow each other.

**`art.fallback_matte` catches a shape the table doesn't name, and a run reports every time it did.** An album holds more shapes than anyone wants to configure, so a stray screenshot takes the fallback rather than stopping the run, and `SyncReport.fell_back` counts them by ratio so the report can name the key worth adding. The fallback itself has to be one of the three that fit anything, since by definition it lands on shapes nobody anticipated.

**`load_config` refuses a matte the TV couldn't draw around its own key's shape**, at load time rather than at upload time, because a wrong one reaches the panel as a crash and a scheduled job would hit it with nobody watching. Every key names its shape, so the whole check can happen there. `tv.upload()` checks the same rule again against the real pixels, because a config is not the only way a matte reaches the TV.

**`sync.delete_removed_from_album` and `sync.delete_added_by_hand` are independent, and they default to the mirror this tool has always been.** The first is on and is what makes a sync a mirror; off, a photo that leaves the album keeps its image and its entry, which also freezes it, because the album no longer holds a copy to re-render from. The second is off and is the only thing that widens a delete past the inventory, which is how a photo added from a phone or one stranded by a timed-out upload is ever reached. Neither can touch Samsung's own art: `sync.unmanaged_uploads()` admits an image only when every row `available()` returns for it says `content_type: mobile` and its id doesn't carry the `SAM-` prefix, so an unfamiliar type is protected rather than guessed at. `load_config` refuses `short_run` paired with the first flag off, because a short run works by mirroring the album down and would otherwise leave everything it dropped on the wall. **A list in `SyncPlan` that no flag names is acted on unconditionally**, which is what keeps a duplicate entry, or a copy superseded by a re-render, from being kept by a flag that was answering a different question.

**`[[pipeline.crop]]` is a rule table keyed on the shape of the photo coming in, not on one target shape for everything.** A rule says a photo of about `when` shape becomes about `to` shape, anchored at `anchor`, and the first rule a photo matches wins, so file order is what settles an overlap. `to = "none"` is how a shape says leave me alone, which is a value rather than the absence of a rule so that "don't crop a portrait" can sit next to the rule that does crop a landscape. **Matching is a 2% tolerance and has to be**, because the album's real ratios sit up to 0.4% off the names anybody would write; the nearest shape it could wrongly catch is 3:2, 12.5% away. `load_config` refuses a rule that buries a later one, since a table read top to bottom makes that easy and nothing else would ever say so. `CropRule.shadows()` is what decides it, and `landscape` sitting above `4:3` is the case worth knowing, because it reads as narrowing and is really burial. `crop.py` is pure and tested and `pipeline.prepare()` is the only thing that applies it.

**`[pipeline.crop_overrides]` names one photo by its source id, or a unique prefix of one, and beats every rule.** The reason to write one is that a rule got that photo wrong, so it wins outright rather than merging. A key has to be at least eight characters, and one sitting inside another is refused, both at load time; whether a key names one photo, several, or none depends on what the source listed, so `frame sync` reports those per run rather than failing. For a Google album the id is the `/photo/` segment of a photo's own URL in the shared album, and it is scoped to that share -- the same photo under a different share link has a different id, so an override copied from anywhere else silently matches nothing.

**`frame sync --label` burns the rule that fired and the first twelve characters of the id into the middle of each photo**, which is what makes a crop judgeable off the panel: a photo that lost a quarter of its height still looks like a photo, and the picker shows no names. Twelve characters because every Google id opens with the same `AF1Qip` and ten is already unique across the album. It is a flag rather than a config key so it can't be left on by accident, and the label is part of the JPEG, so settling the rules means one more run without it. `pipeline.label_center()` is shared with the bakeoff.

**`sync.short_run` narrows a run to the newest N photos of each orientation, and it still mirrors,** so everything it leaves out is deleted off the TV. That is the point of it rather than a hazard to work around: it exists so that trying a matte or a color on the wall costs a couple of minutes instead of an album, and leaving the other photos up there would give you nothing to judge the handful against. Newest is `taken_at_ms` off the source rather than the order a source listed its items in, with the source id breaking a tie so two runs over an unchanged album pick the same photos. `sync.newest_per_orientation()` is pure and tested; the CLI applies it after the empty-source refusal, so a scraper that returned nothing is still caught first.


## The bakeoff

Choosing a mat is done by looking at the panel, and `frame bakeoff` is what puts something there to look at. It uploads the newest photo of each shape the album holds, once per matte, holding everything else still, so the mat is the only thing that differs between the copies of one photo.

**A round covers every shape rather than one photo, because the shape decides which types the TV will draw.** Three of the six are refused on anything but a 16:9, so a round run on a 4:3 says nothing about the 16:9 beside it in the same album, and a mat chosen on one may not exist for the other. `--orientation` narrows a round, which is worth doing for the color rounds: sixteen colors over three shapes is 48 uploads for a comparison that doesn't turn on shape. A matte can only be set as an image is uploaded, which is why a variant is an upload rather than a setting; `docs/spikes.md` T14 is why `change_matte()` isn't the cheap way round that.

Three rounds, in this order: colors on a landscape, colors on a portrait, then one type round over every shape, in the color the first two settled. Colors come first, which means they are judged under a type held constant, and `bakeoff.COLOR_ROUND_TYPE` is `flexible` because the TV draws it around any shape and its aperture takes the image's own, so nothing is cropped underneath the color being looked at. **The consequence is worth remembering: if a type round lands on something other than `flexible`, the color was chosen under a different type and deserves one confirming look rather than a re-run.** The colors go up lightest first, off the triples `get_matte_list()` publishes, so that near neighbours sit next to each other where the comparison is actually hard.

**The name of whatever varies, and the shape it is drawn around, go into the middle of the image, and that is the only thing on the panel that says which copy is which.** A color round burns `polar   4:3` and a type round burns `modernwide   16:9`, rather than the whole matte id, most of which is the half being held constant. The shape is in there because a round puts several different photos up and two copies of one matte on two shapes are otherwise indistinguishable. The picker shows thumbnails and no names, `get_current()` follows a selection rather than the picker's cursor, and counting positions with a remote is a mistake nobody would notice. It also survives into a photograph of the panel, which is what T13 lacked. `pipeline.label_center()` draws it at a fixed 40px, white over a dark stroke so it reads on any photo, and it is never reached by the sync path.

**`[bakeoff]` narrows a round, and an empty list or a missing key means every matte.** An empty list reads the same as an absent key on purpose, so the key can sit in a config being edited rather than having to be deleted and retyped. `colors` and `types` name what may go on the wall, validated at load time against `mattes.py` so a name the firmware has no record of is refused before a round starts. One `types` list serves every shape and what the TV will draw narrows it, so `modernwide` can sit in it for the sake of a 16:9 and simply not appear in a 4:3 round; a list that narrows a shape down to nothing is what gets refused, rather than that shape being quietly skipped. `candidate_labels()` is the offline half of that, and it exists so every image can be drawn before the channel opens: a label carries the varying name and the shape, and nothing about the matte id.

**A round empties the TV first, and that delete is wider than anything a sync does:** it takes every uploaded image, whether or not the inventory claims it, because a leftover sitting between the variants in the picker turns a left-and-right comparison into a hunt. Samsung's art is never a candidate, which `sync.unmanaged_uploads()` enforces. The command names what it is about to delete and asks, unless `--yes`. The question is asked with the art channel open, which closes itself after about 25 seconds of silence, so it comes before any other work: a channel that dies waiting for an answer has deleted nothing.

Uploads are attributed to a `bakeoff` source rather than the album's, and that is what keeps a sync away from them, since a sync scopes its deletes to the entries its own source owns. `[art.matte_by_ratio]`, `art.fallback_matte`, `sync.short_run` and both delete flags are ignored for the length of a round, because a round settles all of them itself.

**`[[pipeline.crop]]` is ignored too, and a round says so when any is configured.** A round holds everything but the mat still, and a crop is the one thing that would change what sits underneath it. It would also make a color round unreadable, since a landscape cropped to the panel's own shape leaves `flexible` barely a hairline of mat to judge a color on. Ignoring crops is also what makes a round's shapes the album's own rather than the config's. **Crops are judged the other way round, through `sync.short_run` and `frame sync --label` rather than a bakeoff**, because a bakeoff puts one photo on the wall many times over and what a crop rule needs is the opposite: a lot of different photos, once each, since the whole question is how the rule lands across them. Restoring the album afterwards is `frame bakeoff --clear` and then a plain `frame sync`.


## Expensive things to know

* **Nothing in this API gives an image a name, and `image_date` is not a way around that.** `available()` reports exactly nine fields per image and none of them is a name or a title, and the `send_image` request carries only a file type, a size, the two matte ids, and `image_date`. That last one is the only text an upload sets, so it looked like a way to make a bakeoff variant identifiable from the TV's own screens. It isn't: the firmware parses it rather than displaying it, so sending a matte name is accepted and stored as the epoch, and both date lines in the picker then read 1970 in place of a real date. `FrameTv.upload()` takes `date` for the sake of a real one; a label has to go into the pixels, which is what `pipeline.label_center()` is for.

These cost real time to find out and are invisible from the code.

* **A Google `lh3` image URL is useless without a size suffix, and `-c` is not a center crop.** The bare URL returns a 512x384 thumbnail. A plain `=wN-hM` fits the frame inside that box and crops nothing, already rotated and carrying the original's own color profile, for a fraction of the bytes of the original. The two that fill the box instead both crop, and `-c` is the trap: `-n` is an exact center crop, while `-c` crops a quarter of the way down from the top, which on every photo tested was a fixed bias rather than anything saliency-aware. Neither is asked for here, because cropping happens in the pipeline where it can be anchored and where it carries to a source that isn't Google. `docs/spikes.md` G4 has the full table and how the crop offsets were pinned down.
* **The box asked for is `=w1920-h1920`, and it is square on purpose.** A photo has to arrive with enough pixels to survive the crop the pipeline is about to take. `=w1920-h1080` returns a 4:3 at 1440x1080, and cropping that to 16:9 gives 1440x810, which the panel then upscales; the square box returns 1920x1440 instead and the same crop lands at a native 1920x1080. Measured against the live album: a 4:3 comes back 1920x1440 at 499 KB against 328 KB for the panel-shaped box, and a 3:4 comes back 1440x1920 at 337 KB against 132 KB. Both halves of the box have to reach 1920, since a portrait spends its long edge on height.
* **A sized `lh3` URL preserves the original's color profile, so a wide-gamut photo is safe to ask for.** Google's resizer passes the pixel numbers through and keeps the ICC profile, so `Display P3` in gives `Display P3` out, and the tag always describes the pixels honestly rather than claiming sRGB for something that isn't. `_to_srgb()` in `pipeline.py` therefore converts correctly, and an untagged original is read as sRGB, which is the convention. The album already holds seven wide-gamut items off an iPhone, so this isn't hypothetical. One wrinkle: for an sRGB source the resizer rewrites the profile name to its own `sRGB IEC61966-2-1 black scaled` flavor while leaving the numbers alone, so a changed profile name is not evidence of a changed image. `docs/spikes.md` G4b has the measurements.
* **Almost no photo in an album of phone photography is 16:9, so filling the panel almost always costs a crop.** Measured 2026-09-06 over the current 237-item album: 161 are about 4:3, 75 are about 3:4, and exactly one is 16:9. The 174-item album before it had none at all. **About, not exactly, and that is what forces a tolerance in two places:** the landscape ratios in there are 4:3 and 85:64 and 1599:1204, the portrait ones 3:4 and 64:85 and 154:205, every one within 0.4% of 4:3 or 3:4. A crop rule matching on equality would match almost nothing, which is `crop.RATIO_TOLERANCE`, and a matte key written `4:3` would miss most of the album, which is why `mattes.ratio_of()` snaps through `limit_denominator(16)` before a key is looked up. So there is no framing that both fills the panel and keeps the whole photo, and the choice is which to give up: a 4:3 cropped to 16:9 loses a quarter of its height, while a 4:3 framed whole spans three quarters of the panel's width, so at least a quarter of the panel becomes mat and more than that if `flexible` insets a landscape the way it insets a portrait, which is unmeasured. Both halves of that choice are config -- `[[pipeline.crop]]` for the crop and `[art.matte_by_ratio]` for the mat -- and they differ in that a matte can be changed by re-uploading while a crop is gone from the stored bytes for good. A 3:4 portrait center-cropped to 16:9 keeps only 42% of the frame height, which is what the crop looks like at its worst.
* **A matte is what makes the TV frame an image; without one it center-crops.** Storage and display do different things, and only storage is visible from the API. `upload()` caps the long edge at 1920 and preserves the aspect ratio, so a 4032x3024 is stored as 1920x1440 and an 810x1080 is stored untouched -- a size bound, not a framing decision. With `matte=none` the panel center-crops anything that isn't 16:9 to fill, never letterboxing and never stretching, and a portrait loses 58% of its height. Given a matte the TV accepts, it instead frames the image whole inside the mat, uncropped and unstretched, with mat on all four sides -- an 810x1080 portrait under `flexible_black` came out at about 86% of the panel's height, so roughly 7% of it is mat above and below. `docs/spikes.md` T9 has the crop photographs, T12 the matted ones, and T16 the measurement.
* **A shared album's `key` query parameter is required.** The public page is `https://photos.google.com/share/<album_id>?key=<key>`, and requesting it without `key` is a 404, so config has to hold the whole link rather than the album id.
* **A Cloudflare Worker can't reach the TV.** The Frame is the server: it listens on `8001` and `8002` on the LAN and your script dials out to it. There is no outbound polling and nothing to configure on the TV, so the push has to originate on the LAN. If sync logic moves to a Worker, the split is that the Worker fetches, preps, and exposes today's images at a stable URL, and a small local job pulls that and pushes. The brains can move; the push can't.
* **The API accepts matte types the TV can't draw, and they crash Art Mode. The rule is keyed to the image's aspect ratio, not its orientation.** This is the hazard the whole matte design has to respect. `get_matte_list()` returns ten types whatever the image, and the TV says nothing about which apply. **`modernthin`, `modern` and `modernwide` draw a fixed 16:9 aperture and are refused on every other shape; `flexible`, `shadowbox` and `none` are accepted on any shape; `panoramic`, `triptych`, `mix` and `squares` are drawn around nothing at all.** A refusal is a Samsung dialog on the panel -- "An unexpected problem has occurred. Please turn off and on and then try again. (40000)" -- which kills the art channel and needs a power cycle. Observed for `modernwide_polar` on an 810x1080 portrait, then for all three fixed-aperture types on a 1434x1080 landscape, twice, in two different colors. Orientation looked like the axis only because every landscape anyone had tested was 1920x1080. **Pass `matte` explicitly always, and refuse anything the TV wouldn't draw around that image's ratio.** `docs/spikes.md` T15 has how the names were read and T22 has the correction.
* **`none` sits in `ACCEPTED_ON_ANY_SHAPE` because it never crashes, not because it fits.** It draws no mat at all and lets the panel center-crop to fill, which costs a 3:4 portrait 58% of its height, so it is a framing choice rather than a hazard. The picker withholds it on a portrait, so a portrait can't be un-matted from the TV itself, but T9 uploaded one that way and got the crop rather than a dialog. That is one observation, and it is the only reason the type is permitted there.
* **A matte's aperture is what the image gets scaled into, so the type is a framing choice and a safety one.** The mat is drawn inside the panel and the image fits its aperture, which on a 32" screen is real picture to give up. The aperture's shape is also what splits the types in two. A fixed aperture imposes its own shape and has nowhere to put anything else: `modernwide` is wider than 16:9 and cut a 1920x1080 top and bottom to fit, and handed a 4:3 the firmware falls over rather than letterboxing. `flexible` instead takes the image's own aspect ratio, so it crops nothing whatever the shape, which is why the TV reaches for it on a portrait. **`flexible` and `shadowbox` are therefore the only pair safe to apply without knowing an image's shape;** anything else has to be weighed per orientation. How much they cost differs a lot: `flexible` on a 16:9 leaves only a hairline of mat, while on a 3:4 portrait it takes about 7% of the panel's height on each of the four sides. It also draws a white bevel about 6 panel pixels wide just inside the aperture, which is real picture on a 32" screen. On color, `black` measures about RGB 30,27,32 off a daylit photograph against the 34,34,33 it declares, so the triples can be trusted in daylight; the earlier readings of it as medium blue and of `polar` as light blue both came from a dark room, which makes the camera the likely culprit rather than the panel. Only the `black` color has been on a portrait, though there is no reason yet to think color and orientation interact. `docs/spikes.md` T15 and T16 have the measurements, and T13 there is what remains.
* **`portrait_matte_id` is inert on this panel; `matte_id` is the one that acts.** Both `upload()` and `change_matte()` carry the pair, and it appears keyed to the panel's orientation rather than the image's, for the models that take the auto-rotating wall mount. On a fixed-landscape 32" the portrait one is never read: an 810x1080 uploaded with `portrait_matte_id=modernwide_polar` displayed cropped with no mat at all, while the same image matted through `matte_id` from the TV's UI framed correctly. The one place it has ever come back as anything but `none` is the Art Store's own `SAM-` stream image.
* **`set_favourite()` does not work on this firmware,** so favourites can't be a playlist and `MY-C0004` has never been observed. The underlying request is `change_favorite`. Its first call returned `error -7`; every one since has hung and taken the art channel with it. `get_thumbnail` hangs the same way, which means `available()`'s reported dimensions are the only machine-readable account of what the TV stored and anything about display has to be photographed.
* **Only one process may hold the art channel at a time,** because the TV's Device List keys on the client `name` and a second connection under the same name tears the first one down. Everything that touches the channel takes an exclusive `fcntl.flock` on `/tmp/frame-tv-art-sync.lock` first, waits rather than failing, and gives up after 90s naming the holder. It lives in `/tmp` rather than beside the token so that no worktree ends up with an untracked lock file in it. This matters for the nightly `launchd` job as much as for two people at two terminals.
* **A long run of uploads has knocked the Art app over once, at 93 of them back to back.** The 94th never answered and the 120s deadline cut the connection. The TV was found afterward playing a live TV show, with nobody having touched the remote during the run: the Art picker was still reachable, but going to AMP from it dropped straight back to the TV show, and only a reboot cleared it. So the Art app can leave AMP on its own under sustained load, and it lands somewhere the API can't recover from. Nothing distinguishes cause from coincidence yet, which is why `syncer` times every upload and prints the series: times climbing toward the deadline would mean the app wearing down, while a flat series ending in one hang would mean a single event. `tv.upload_pause` exists as the cheap mitigation for the first reading and is off by default, because there is no evidence yet that it helps. `docs/TODO.md` T18 tracks it.
* **A wedged Art app is a fifth state, and it looks like none of the other four.** REST reports `PowerState: on`, so the set is awake rather than in standby, while the art channel accepts a connection and then never completes the handshake, timing out on `connect`. The panel is black or drawing partially. Nothing over this API recovers it, because every call needs the channel that is stuck, so it takes a power cycle at the wall. It has happened twice, once mid-upload and once on opening the picker over 183 images, and `docs/TODO.md` T18 and T20 hold what is known. Treat a `connect` timeout against a TV that answers REST as this rather than as a network problem.
* **A slideshow the API refuses to start may still be settable by hand.** T5 proved only that `set_slideshow_status` takes `duration=0` and nothing else; it did not prove the TV can't rotate, and it obviously can, since it does it for the Art Store and the setting is in its own UI. Because the TV keeps its own state after the script disconnects, a slideshow turned on from the menu may simply keep running, which would make the whole rotation problem disappear. That's `docs/TODO.md` T21, and it comes before any code that selects images on a timer.
* **Uploading never changes what the panel shows, and nothing else here does either.** 86 uploads went by with the panel sitting on the image it started on, which makes a sync visually silent and a nightly job safe to run over a lit wall. It also means the wall never changes on its own: a slideshow can be turned off and never on, so without something selecting an image the TV shows one photo forever. `select_image(content_id)` is in `samsungtvws` and is used nowhere here yet; a scheduled job calling it is the slideshow this firmware refuses to run, and the rotation half of this project rests on it. `docs/TODO.md` T19.
* **An upload that times out may still have landed, so a failed run can leave an image only one flag can delete.** The bytes go out over the D2D socket and only the confirmation comes back on the art channel, so losing the channel loses the `content_id` rather than the upload. It happened on the first real sync: the 94th upload timed out, and `frame status` afterward reported 97 images against an inventory of 93, the extra being that photo. An image in that state is indistinguishable from art added by hand, so it stays until it is removed from the TV's own picker or a run with `sync.delete_added_by_hand` takes it, which is the case that key exists for. `syncer` names the photo that was in flight when a run dies, which is the only way to know to go looking.
* **Every upload opens its own TCP connection to the TV, on top of the art channel.** `api_version` is `5.0.0.0` and `get_api_version` goes unanswered on this firmware, so `samsungtvws` takes the D2D socket path rather than the `0.97` binary one: it asks over the websocket, the TV answers with an address and port, and the library connects, sends the bytes, and closes. An album is therefore that many separate connections, which is the leading suspect in T18 and the reason a pause between uploads is worth trying at all.
* **`--debug` is the only way to see a frame nothing asked for.** `samsungtvws` matches replies by `request_id` and drops everything else, so an unsolicited `art_mode_changed` announcing that the TV left art mode is invisible at any other log level. It reaches the library's own `DEBUG` logging, which is what the flag turns on.
* **An idle art channel closes inside 25 seconds.** Anything holding a connection while waiting has to poll, `get_current()` being the cheap choice, rather than sleep.
* **Mattes are per-photo, they can only be set at upload time, and the TV has no global matte setting.** A matte id is a type and a color joined together, so `shadowbox_polar` is type `shadowbox` in color `polar`, and `get_matte_list()` returns the two lists separately. Each list holds tables rather than names -- `{'matte_type': 'flexible'}` and `{'color': 'black', 'R': 34, 'G': 34, 'B': 33}`, the channel keys upper case where the name's key is lower -- which is easy to miss, because printing them looks right up until every id turns out to be the string form of a dict. Ten types and sixteen colors come back, all of them for every image, and the colors carry their triples, so `frame mattes` is where to read what a color is meant to be. `upload(matte=, portrait_matte=)` is the only call that sets one. `change_matte()` looks like the way to restyle an image already on the TV and does nothing at all: it refuses every image with `error -7`, a landscape and a request that asks for no change included, and `available()` reports the old value afterward. `docs/spikes.md` T14 has the four calls. So every upload carries the matte `[art.matte_by_ratio]` gives its shape, and restyling anything already up there means re-uploading it, which mints a new `content_id` and rewrites its inventory entry. That is what the render record turns into a diff: `frame sync` compares each entry's recorded matte against what config now asks for and replaces the ones that differ.
* **A slideshow can be turned off over this API and cannot be turned on.** `set_slideshow_status` accepts `duration=0` and refuses every non-zero duration with `error -7`, whatever the category, shuffle flag, or interval, and whether or not `sub_category_id` is supplied. Nine variants were tried. The TV plainly can run one, since it rotates the Art Store on its own, so the capability exists and is simply unreachable this way. `docs/spikes.md` T5 has the list. Categories are still worth knowing: `MY-C0002` is My Pictures, `MY-C0004` Favourites, `MY-C0008` the Store. Two more turn up that no documentation mentions -- `ARTSTREAM` on the free Art Store rotation, and `MY-C0009`, which `get_current()` reported for an uploaded photo that `available()` filed under `MY-C0002`. So the set is open-ended and nothing should reject an unrecognized one.
* **There are two near-identical slideshow endpoints, and neither can start a slideshow here.** `set_slideshow_status` answers but takes only `off`. `set_auto_rotation_status` never answers at all, getter or setter, and hangs the run rather than erroring, so calling it costs a connection. Both were tested; don't let the next person assume the auto-rotation pair is untried.
* **Port `8002` is the only one that works.** `8001` is unencrypted and tokenless and doesn't do art mode on recent firmware. The first connection pops an on-screen "Allow this device?" prompt that has to be accepted within about 30 seconds.
* **The TV only accepts WebSocket clients from its own subnet, paired or not.** From another VLAN it answers `ms.channel.timeOut` in well under a second with no prompt, for an already-allowed client exactly as for an unknown one, while REST keeps answering. So whatever runs `frame` has to have an address on the TV's subnet; `docs/network.md` has the network consequences and `docs/spikes.md` T2c the test.
* **Never send a token on the art channel, and don't reach it through `SamsungTVWS.art()`.** The art channel doesn't issue a token, but `samsungtvws` sends whatever is in `token_file` on every channel, and a token the TV no longer recognises, which is what clearing Device List produces, makes the art channel accept the upgrade and then stay silent instead of refusing. `art()` copies the remote object's own `token_file` onto the art channel, so the only way to be sure is to construct `SamsungTVArt(host, port=8002, token_file=None, name=...)` directly, which is what `tv.py` does. The token file belongs to the remote channel alone.
* **Reconnecting with the same client name within about 10s of the last connection breaks the handshake.** The TV is slow to notice a client left, and the next connection either gets silence or receives the old session's `ms.channel.clientDisconnect` as its first frame, which `samsungtvws` reads as a failed connect. Hold one connection per invocation, and back off by tens of seconds before any retry. `_connect` does that back-off itself, so two commands in a row cost a wait rather than a failure.
* **`get_artmode_settings()` is where the valid ranges live, and asking for one setting is the readable way in.** The whole payload's `data` field is JSON inside a string, so it needs a second `json.loads`; passing a setting name lets the library do that and hand back the one entry, `{"item": "brightness", "value": "4", "min": "0", "max": "10"}`, with every number a string as usual. `brightness` is 0-10, `color_temperature` -5 to 5, `motion_sensitivity` 1-3, and `motion_timer` an enum under `valid_values` rather than a range. Validate against what it reports rather than a constant, because nothing else publishes them.
* **The same image reports a different `content_type` depending on which call you ask.** An uploaded photo is `mobile` in `available()` and `myphoto` in `get_current()`, on top of `server` for the Store stream, `artstore` for that same stream seen from `get_current()`, and `preinstall` for the bundled art. So `content_type` describes the view as much as the item, and joining the two calls on it, or on `category_id`, gives nothing.
* **`available()` is a concatenation of per-category listings, not a flat list.** The same `content_id` comes back once per category it appears under, with a different `category_id` on each row, so a response can hold the same upload twice. Dedupe on `content_id` before anything else, and never decide whether an item is yours from its `category_id` -- that is what the inventory file is for.
* **`available()` includes the live Art Store stream image,** a `SAM-...` id with `content_type: server` that changes as the stream rotates. The sync diff has to filter those out or it will see phantom adds and deletes every run. Not every `SAM-` row is `server`, though: a second one reports `content_type: preinstall` and a stable id, so the filter leaves it in and it shows up under `unmanaged` on every dry run. That's harmless, because it never rotates and the `SAM-` prefix keeps it out of everything `sync.delete_added_by_hand` reaches, but don't read its presence there as a bug. The `server` rows also arrive twice under one `category_id`, differing only in their `slideshow` field, which is one more reason to dedupe on `content_id` before anything else.
* **The two channels pair separately, and only one issues a token.** The art channel's connect frame carries no token; its authorization is the Device List entry, keyed on the client `name`. The `samsung.remote.control` channel shows its own Allow prompt and does issue a token, which is what the token file holds. `frame pair` has to open both, because the power key for the nightly `off` lives on the remote channel. Keep `name` constant, because that's the identity in Device List, and expect one more prompt whenever the controlling machine's IP changes, because Device List keeps one entry per name and address; a client moving between Wi-Fi and Ethernet hits this, and so does a DHCP lease change, so the controlling machine needs a reservation.
* **The art channel blocks forever rather than refusing when it won't answer, and only cutting the socket ends the wait.** `samsungtvws` waits on `ms.channel.ready` with no timeout of its own, and the socket `timeout` it accepts does not end a blocked read either -- one outlasted a 45s timeout indefinitely, apparently because the TV keeps the socket busy enough that it is never idle long enough to trip. So `tv.py` arms a watchdog thread per request that calls `shutdown(SHUT_RDWR)` on the socket underneath the blocked read, which makes it raise; the exception depends on the transport, `BrokenPipeError` over TLS and `WebSocketConnectionClosedException` without it, so a timeout is recognized by the watchdog having fired rather than by the type. `.claude/tmp/shutdown_probe.py` is what proved that, against a local server that completes the handshake and then says nothing. Doing it this way rather than with the spikes' `os._exit` watchdog is what lets a failed `frame sync` still write `inventory.json`. The one window it can't cover is the handshake, before the library exposes the socket, where the library's own socket timeout is the only bound; no hang has been observed there, and bounding it would mean running the call on a worker thread joined with the deadline. Which screens it answers from is less clear than it looked: uploads, deletes and slideshow requests all worked in a run where the TV was on the Art menu rather than in Art Mode proper, so the earlier reading that only Art Mode proper works needs re-testing before anything relies on it.
* **Pairing fails silently if Access Notification is off.** It's under Settings > General > External Device Manager > Device Connect Manager. To re-pair, clear the entry from Device List in the same menu first, or the TV remembers the denial and won't prompt again.
* **Firmware updates reset things.** Tizen updates have flipped Access Notification back and invalidated tokens. Failures need to log loudly, because a scheduled job that silently no-ops for weeks is the realistic outcome otherwise.
* **`art.upload()` returns the new `content_id`,** which is the inventory key. It also normalizes a `file_type` of `jpeg` to `jpg`.
* **The dark panel is the remote's long-press "off", and the TV stays fully awake behind it.** It answers every art request and `get_artmode()` still says `on`, so the art channel can't tell lit from dark. REST `PowerState` can: `on` is lit, `standby` is dark. Read that for `frame status` and before deciding whether to wake.
* **`set_artmode(False)` is "exit to the TV UI", not "screen off".** On a lit TV it drops to the last input or the Tizen home screen. No art-channel call darkens the panel; that takes a held `KEY_POWER` on the `samsung.remote.control` channel, which needs its own pairing prompt. `docs/spikes.md` T2d tracks it.
* **Waking a dark panel is a toggle, not a call.** `set_artmode(True)` alone hangs with no reply and changes nothing. `set_artmode(False)` followed a few seconds later by `set_artmode(True)` lights it, and REST `PowerState` flips to `on` on the second call. `frame art-mode on` has to do that two-step whenever REST says `standby`.


## Development process

Spikes come first, and each one's output is a written finding rather than code you keep. `docs/spikes.md` has the runnable detail for each: what to find out, how to find it out, and where the answer gets recorded. `docs/TODO.md` is the checkbox list that tracks them.

The spikes split into two groups that don't depend on each other, so they can happen in any order or in parallel. The Google scraper probe needs no TV at all. The TV probes need the TV on the network and a one-time interactive pairing, and they should stay read-only until pairing is confirmed working.

After the spikes, build from the inside out. The pipeline's size math and the sync diff are pure functions with no hardware in them, so write their tests first. The TV wrapper gets verified by hand against the real TV instead, because mocking an undocumented WebSocket protocol would only prove the mock matches the code.

Throwaway spike scripts go in `.claude/tmp/`. Nothing in there is a deliverable.


## Conventions

* **`AMP` means Art Mode proper**, the panel lit and displaying art. It is worth a name because the TV has four states that all get called "art mode" loosely and behave differently: `AMP`; the Art menu, which is the picker UI and answers art requests too; the TV UI, which is where `set_artmode(False)` lands; and the dark panel, which REST reports as `standby` while the art channel still says `on`. Say `AMP` in notes and commit messages rather than spelling it out.
* Follow standard Python conventions and match the surrounding code. Type hints throughout, since `samsungtvws` ships `py.typed`.
* Write tests for meaningful behavior, especially the sync diff and the pipeline's size math. Don't chase coverage.
* Keep `docs/TODO.md` and `docs/spikes.md` current as work lands. A spike that answered its question should have its finding written down and then be referenced from CLAUDE.md if it changes a design assumption.
* Comments explain why, not what.
